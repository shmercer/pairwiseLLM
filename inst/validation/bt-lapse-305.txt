Frozen design
  Complete graphs: 4, 8, 12 items.
  Sparse graphs: 8 and 12 items, paths closed into cycles with fixed chords.
  Every edge has both presentation directions and 2,000 observations per direction.
  Centered theta spans [-2,2]; beta is 0, -0.3, or 0.3.
  Epsilon is 0.001, 0.05, 0.2, or 0.4, plus separate true-zero boundary cases.
  Each cell has a population expected-count oracle and five integer-binomial
  samples with fixed seeds 30501 through 30505. Integer counts are the sufficient
  statistics for repeated binary observations; public row aggregation is tested
  independently against these counts.
  Population recovery: maximum absolute error <= 1e-5 for theta, beta, epsilon.
  Seeded recovery: maximum absolute theta/beta/epsilon errors <= 0.35/0.15/0.08.
  Both a locally valid fit AND those recovery tolerances are required to pass.

All 60 positive-epsilon population cases recovered their known parameters;
the maximum errors were theta 3.235e-12, beta 9.698e-14, epsilon 1.314e-12.
The 15 true-zero population cases were rejected as boundary fits.
Of 375 seeded cases, 264 returned valid fits and all met the recovery tolerances.
Their maximum absolute errors were theta 0.299931, beta 0.101371, epsilon 0.033979.

The nonstationary case was the complete 12-item graph, true beta 0,
true epsilon 0.001, seed 30501. Its natural-coordinate maximum score was 93.15404
despite the optimizer's convergence code. It was rejected; no tolerance was
relaxed and no alternative solution was substituted to obtain uncertainty.
Twenty-five other near-zero cases retained the best candidate's native failure
to converge. These are limitations of this fixed implementation, not proof that
all possible unpenalized algorithms must fail.

What the evidence establishes
  Recovery across the specified graph sizes, including zero bias and connected
  sparse graphs, succeeded for all cases with true epsilon 0.05, 0.2, and 0.4.
  All positive-epsilon population cases passed. No boundary pathologies were
  observed in the three specified moderate-lapse settings.
  Natural/logit derivatives agree with finite differences. Joint covariance
  agrees with an independent orthonormal-coordinate oracle and includes beta
  and epsilon estimation. Relabeling, row-order and presentation-reversal tests
  passed. Predictions agree with independent hand calculations and the current
  Bayesian likelihood formula; no Stan sampler was run.
  Nonidentification, separation, disconnected graphs, bad curvature, boundary
  and optimizer failures are retained without SEs, covariance repair or fallback.

What remains unestablished
  Stable near-zero estimation and ordinary joint uncertainty are not established.
  Correct rejection at epsilon zero is not successful regular joint estimation.
  Positive curvature and a recovery pass do not prove repeated-sample SE/interval
  calibration, global optimality over all modes, or validity on other graphs.
  This audit is not a study-specific power/coverage analysis. Conventional SSR
  remains unavailable for every lapse fit. Adaptive bootstrap remains out of scope.
