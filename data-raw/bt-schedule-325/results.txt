Issue #325 measured results

The measured change reuses the canonical adaptive loop and its validated setup
across bounded blocks (at most 64 attempted comparisons, bounded further by
initialization, round, native-refit and remaining-budget boundaries). Every
comparison and native refit still runs. A completed-iteration observer preserves
the original partial state when a later refit fails. The new optional ordered
executor permits caller-owned pool reuse; summary/theta chunks reuse #328's
prepared designs across up to eight replicates. Full chunks remain bounded at
two. Outcome counts and numerical checks remain per replicate; dense designs
are constructed in workers and never dispatched from the parent.

Baseline findings before implementation
  Nine complete profiles: N=57,91,229 x B=4,8,16, p50/alpha, two replicates each.
  Disjoint elapsed stack samples: controller/state/log/checkpoint handling
  55.1-61.7%; four-chain native refits 16.2-27.7%; repeated validation 5.1-8.3%;
  pair selection 2.6-4.1%; TrueSkill updates 0.1-0.3%; cloning/hash/serialization
  0.3-0.5%. Final estimation ranges from 0.1% to 16.2% (N229/B4).
  Thus state/controller work dominates these workloads. Cloning pristine inputs
  is small and was left intact. Bootstrap disables persistent checkpoints, so
  this category contains state/log work and checkpoint decisions, not disk I/O.
  Separate final-fitting components cover both alpha and Firth in all nine cells.
  N229 alpha retained design: 144,420,888 bytes (137.7 MiB; approximately 139 MiB).
  Its final prepared fits cost 8.951-10.942 seconds each in these measurements;
  Firth costs 1.894-4.492 seconds. These timings reuse actual simulated outcomes.
  The optimized B4 profiles retain 49.8-56.2% in state/controller handling,
  18.3-29.0% in native fits, and 5.9-6.4% in dynamic validation. These are
  descriptive stack fractions, not additional paired throughput estimates.
  Transport microprofiles use actual-size specs/results. At N229 the input spec
  serializes to 104,664 bytes and a compact result to 6,302 bytes. Two-worker
  initialization/namespace loading takes 1.028 s; twelve-worker loading 1.140 s.
  Ten twelve-way input roundtrips take 8.513 s, compact result roundtrips 0.823 s;
  100 ordered reductions of actual results take 0.123 s. These isolate framework
  overhead and do not replace complete-workload rates.
  Native fit duration is also measured directly; stack categories are disjoint
  and no inclusive profiler totals are added together.

Limits and remaining risks
  The changes reduce repeated setup; substantial controller/phase-A/state work
  remains. They do not claim to eliminate the dominant category. Pool ownership
  is explicit: callers must configure concurrency consistently with workers and
  reserve native-chain resources across all outer tasks. No dependency is added.
  Scientific qualification is bounded and synthetic, with cold native trajectories
  plus deterministic warm/reservoir/retry/positive-stop fixtures. It is not an
  exhaustive native crossproduct of every supported adaptive configuration.
  Firth complete-workload timing uses the diagonal N57/B4, N91/B8, N229/B16;
  all nine N/B cells have isolated final-fitting components, not nine complete
  Firth workloads. Worker measurements use summary retention; serial qualification
  uses full retention. These rates must not be pooled into one scaling curve.
  The native hybrid/Firth N57/B4 fixture preserves its original one-success,
  one-failure result. Successful-replicate rates exclude failed estimates; no
  replacement replicate, relaxed threshold, fallback fitter or retry is used.
  Two seeded calls amortize startup in the worker grid, but these remain single
  bounded-cell point estimates. Serial grids overlapped other qualification/check
  jobs; recorded host load and timing variability limit small speedup comparisons.
  Worker-grid jobs run sequentially without other native benchmark workloads.
  Sum-RSS samples can double-count shared pages and miss transient peaks; CPU
  samples can miss short-lived descendants. Parent/worker/CmdStan measurements
  include sampling/audit overhead in elapsed time. Compilation and pool startup
  are reported separately; the main throughput rate includes pool startup.
  Historical integrated alpha peak RSS can exceed the study package's peak even
  though execution is faster. The retained design alone is not a complete memory
  forecast, and its size does not explain all integrated allocation differences.
  At N229/B4, increasing the optimized pool from 8 to 12 workers raises measured
  throughput from 0.09144 to 0.10832 successful replicates/s (18.5%) while peak
  summed RSS rises from 9.03 to 13.50 GiB (49.6%). This is a bounded measurement,
  not an optimal-production-concurrency claim. At 12 workers, alpha RSS is also
  higher than the post-#328 temporary-pool reference (13.50 versus 9.75 GiB).
  Measurements on this Ryzen 9 5900XT do not select production concurrency.
  Scripts reserve 20% of actual physical CPU capacity, cap live summed RSS at
  70% of MemAvailable, and restrict cells to 2-24 replicates. No production study,
  provider, active study library, stopping threshold or release was changed.
