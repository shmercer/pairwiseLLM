All 1,600 sensitivity fits succeeded and their scientific outputs matched
exactly. The longer jobs reverse the observed N=91 short-job regressions
at 1/12 workers. This supports a workload-length effect, without isolating
the contributions of startup, garbage collection, dispatch and fitting.
Both sets of measurements remain in the evidence; the sensitivity check
does not justify a universal Firth speedup claim.

worker-summary.csv also reports mean/median amortized seconds per fit and
mean successful fits/s. bootstrap.csv contains every run, its elapsed and
parent CPU time, sampled peak RSS and system load. Parent package loading
and a two-item engine warmup are outside the bootstrap timer; process_seconds
records the full fresh R invocation. With two observations,
the median and mean of amortized time are identical; minimum throughput
shows the slower observed run. RSS is sampled every 50 ms over the process
tree/session; shared pages can be counted repeatedly and short peaks missed.
RSS includes startup and workers. Rprofmem totals are cumulative allocations,
not peak live memory. A retained cache does not imply lower peak RSS.

Why these changes:
The original N=229 alpha Rprof sample attributed 78.26% of elapsed time to
the IWLS solver and 19.76% to crossprod. The two standalone stage profiles
averaged 0.005 s for IDs/counts, 0.002 s for contrast/design construction,
0.080 s for alpha structures, 7.637 s for the solver, 1.874 s for uncertainty
and diagnostics, and 0.002 s for result components. Firth's solver alone
averaged 0.588 s against a 0.602 s full fit. These separately profiled calls
include profiling/first-use overhead and must not be added or interpreted as
the final unprofiled speedup. Public example_writing_pairs was also profiled.
Indexed tabulation and the weighted Laplacian avoid repeated aggregation and
zero products. candidates.csv compares isolated aggregate/tabulate/rowsum,
dense/sparse/indexed information, and formula/direct Firth calls. The formula
wrapper remains because removing it did not show a material solver gain and
would change the raw model-object contract. No sparse solver was substituted.

Numerical equivalence and validation:
Frozen source files match the baseline SHA byte for byte. Counts, row order,
scores, solver coefficients/iterations/deviance, flags, classes/reasons and
bootstrap scientific summaries require exact equality. Other floating-point
diagnostics, SE and covariance use the predeclared elementwise tolerance
1e-10*(1+abs(reference)); it was never loosened to obtain passing results.
The 112 extended fit cases span N=8/57/91/229, seven graph families and three
alpha values (0, 1e-10, 0.5) plus Firth. A full reference-BLAS run passed before
the conditioning guard; the final implementation passed all extended cases
under OpenBLAS and the focused suites under both libraries. Full R CMD check
uses reference BLAS. Large-graph design/count oracles also run in routine CI.
OpenBLAS exposed a tiny-alpha near-separated case where 2.22e-16 Hessian
roundoff amplified to 2.2764e-10 in the Newton correction. Fits whose already
computed information/Hessian reciprocal condition estimate is nonfinite or
below 1e-5 now retain the original dense BLAS products. This changes only the
computational path, not an acceptance threshold. The regression requires
exact original diagnostics; conditioning-openblas.csv records zero remaining
difference. Original starts, objective, all-pairs pseudo-counts, centering,
Firth AS_mean, uncertainty, convergence and failure gates remain in force.
equivalence.csv records the measured score/SE/covariance/information errors
for the final component benchmark. Every one of the 120 worker cells requires
24 successes and exact agreement with its baseline serial scientific result:
2,880 fits, including 1/2/4/8/12 workers. Fixed and changing adaptive schedules,
custom callbacks, failures, RNG restoration and immutable cache behavior are
covered by the regression suites.

Remaining limits:
Dense IWLS/QR dominates large-N fitting, especially Firth, so caching cannot
produce a large speedup there. Ill-conditioned alpha fits deliberately retain
the original diagnostic crossproducts. Alpha's cached dense all-pair/binary
designs retain memory per worker. Parallel chunks build designs locally to
avoid transferring dense matrices over sockets; they preserve the existing
2*workers result bound, seeds and reduction order. Startup and scheduling can
dominate short bootstrap jobs. Two repeated runs characterize these synthetic
workloads, not longer production jobs or concurrent study resource demands.
No provider was invoked; no study outcomes, settings, dependencies, pinned
installed library or version were changed. Study qualification/repinning and
merging remain explicit maintainer decisions.
